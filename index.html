Plan Your Trip: Two Routes

Where to go, how to get there, and how to string the sites together. Both routes assume you can take high-speed trains and are comfortable with some long drives.

Tang Heritage Trail

8-10 days · Xi'an → Taiyuan → Xinzhou → Dunhuang

Days 1-3: Xi'an

Big Wild Goose Pagoda, Daming Palace, Shaanxi History Museum. Stay near Bell Tower. Book Shaanxi Museum 3+ days ahead on WeChat.

Day 3: Xi'an to Baoji (day trip)

Famen Temple Museum. 2 hrs by car or 1.5 hrs HSR + taxi. Return to Xi'an.

Day 4: Xi'an to Taiyuan

HSR Xi'an-Taiyuan (3.5 hrs). Afternoon: Jinci Temple. Stay in Taiyuan.

Days 5-6: Xinzhou mountain temples

Hire car+driver from Taiyuan (~600-800 CNY/day). 4 hr mountain road. Visit Foguang East Hall + Nanchan Temple in one day. Overnight in Doucun (basic guesthouse) or return to Taiyuan. No public transit.

Days 7-9: Dunhuang

Fly Taiyuan-Dunhuang or train via Lanzhou. Mogao Caves: book Standard Visit (8 caves, 238 CNY) 2+ weeks ahead on WeChat mini-program. Stay in Dunhuang city.

Day 10: Depart

Fly out from Dunhuang (DNH) or return to Xi'an.

Best season: April-May, September-October. Avoid July-August and Chinese New Year.

Difficulty: Moderate-hard. Mountain roads, remote locations, limited English.

Budget: ~8,000-12,000 CNY/person (excluding international flights).

Song Heritage Trail

10-14 days · Kaifeng → Zhengding → Taiyuan → Suzhou → Hangzhou → Ningbo

Day 1: Arrive Zhengzhou, transfer to Kaifeng

Fly into Zhengzhou (CGO). 40 min HSR to Kaifeng. Afternoon: Iron Pagoda. Stay in Kaifeng.

Day 2: Kaifeng

Qingming Riverside Garden (theme park, family-friendly). Note: Song Kaifeng is buried 8m under silt. Only the Iron Pagoda (1049) is original.

Days 3-4: Zhengding and Taiyuan

HSR to Shijiazhuang (2 hrs), taxi to Zhengding (30 min). Longxing Temple. Next day: HSR to Taiyuan (1.5 hrs). Jinci Temple. Stay in Taiyuan.

Days 5-6: Suzhou

Fly or HSR Taiyuan-Shanghai, train to Suzhou (30 min). Tiger Hill Pagoda, Suzhou Museum. Stay in Suzhou old town.

Days 7-9: Hangzhou

HSR Suzhou-Hangzhou (1 hr). Liuhe Pagoda, Southern Song Official Kiln Museum, West Lake. Stay near West Lake.

Days 10-11: Ningbo

HSR Hangzhou-Ningbo (1 hr). Baoguo Temple Main Hall (1013). Fly out from Ningbo (NGB) or Shanghai (PVG).

Best season: March-May, October-November. Jiangnan is beautiful in spring. Avoid summer humidity.

Difficulty: Easy-moderate. All Jiangnan sites accessible by public transit.

Budget: ~10,000-15,000 CNY/person (excluding international flights).

Tang and Song Food: What to Eat and Where

China's two greatest dynasties also shaped what Chinese people eat today. Tang brought Silk Road ingredients to the table. Song invented the restaurant as we know it. You can still taste both in the cities they once ruled.

Tang Dynasty Food (618–907)

Tang Chang'an was the most cosmopolitan city on earth. Persian merchants, Central Asian traders, and Indian monks all brought their food. Wheat became dominant in the north. The elite drank grape wine, ate lamb, and flavored everything with freshly ground pepper from India.

Hu Food (胡食) — Silk Road Cuisine

Hu bing (胡饼, sesame flatbread from Central Asia) was the Tang equivalent of pizza — sold on every street corner. Bing (pastries and flatbreads) were invented by Turkic and Persian bakers and became Chang'an's street food staple.

Grape Wine and Foreign Drinks

The Tang elite abandoned traditional grain alcohol for grape wine imported from the Western Regions. Tang poets wrote endlessly about wine. Li Bai's most famous poems were composed drunk. Kumiss (fermented mare's milk) was also popular among nobles.

Tea Emerges

Tea drinking spread from Buddhist monasteries to the general population during Tang. Lu Yu wrote The Classic of Tea (《茶经》) around 760, the world's first comprehensive book on tea. By the late Tang, tea was a national obsession.

Song Dynasty Food (960–1279)

The Song invented the restaurant. Before Song, you ate at home or at inns for travelers. The Song commercial revolution created specialized eateries in Kaifeng and Hangzhou — noodle shops, dumpling houses, Sichuan pepper restaurants, vegetarian Buddhist eateries. Street food culture exploded.

The World's First Restaurants

Kaifeng had restaurants open 24 hours, serving hundreds of dishes. Menus existed. Dongjing Meng Hua Lu (《东京梦华录》, "Dreams of Splendor of the Eastern Capital") documents 300+ named dishes available in 12th-century Kaifeng.

Rice Takes Over

New fast-ripening Champa rice from Vietnam doubled yields. Rice became the southern staple. Meanwhile, wheat noodle culture exploded in the north. Mian (noodles) and jiaozi (dumplings) became what they are today.

Dian Cha (点茶) — Whisked Tea Art

The Song elite whisked powdered tea into foam using bamboo whisks, then competed to create the most beautiful froth. This art was later adopted by Buddhist monasteries and spread throughout East Asia. Emperor Huizong wrote a treatise on tea.

Where to Eat Tang and Song Food Today

Xi'an — Taste Tang Dynasty Chang'an

Yang rou pao mo (lamb soup with torn bread, 羊肉泡馍, 30–50 CNY) is Tang-era military food. Rou jia mo (肉夹馍, 10–15 CNY) descends from hu bing. The Muslim Quarter (回民街) carries Tang Silk Road food traditions: lamb skewers, persimmon cakes, pomegranate juice. Defachang Dumpling House near Bell Tower has served dumplings since 1936 — try the dumpling banquet (100–200 CNY).

Kaifeng — Eat Like a Song Emperor

Guan tang bao (灌汤包, soup dumplings, 40–60 CNY per basket at Diyilou, open since 1922) are Kaifeng's defining dish. Tong zi ji (桶子鸡, braised young chicken) and chao liang fen (炒凉粉, fried mung bean jelly) are Song-era street foods still sold at the Drum Tower Night Market. For the full experience, eat at Dongjing Restaurant inside Qingming Riverside Garden for dishes recreated from Song cookbooks.

Hangzhou — Southern Song Capital Flavors

Dongpo rou (东坡肉, braised pork belly, 60–80 CNY) is named after Su Shi (Su Dongpo), the Song poet-official who invented (or popularized) it. Longjing shrimp (龙井虾仁, shrimp stir-fried with Dragon Well tea leaves) and West Lake fish in vinegar sauce (西湖醋鱼) are classic Hangzhou dishes. Louwailou Restaurant (楼外楼, since 1848) on West Lake serves all three.

Suzhou — Jiangnan Refinement

Songshu guiyu (松鼠桂鱼, squirrel-shaped mandarin fish, 120–180 CNY) is the most famous Suzhou dish. Xie fen xiaolong (蟹粉小笼, crab roe soup dumplings) are seasonal, best in autumn. Song He Lou (松鹤楼, since 1757) on Shantang Street serves classic Suzhou banquet cuisine. For street food: sheng jian bao (生煎包, pan-fried pork buns) along Shantang Street.

Traveling in China: Practical Guide for First-Timers

China is not difficult to travel, but it's different. The language barrier is real, and China's digital ecosystem can lock you out before you even land. Here is everything you need to navigate it.

Payments: Alipay and WeChat Pay

Cash is rarely accepted. Download Alipay before your trip and link your international credit card (Visa/Mastercard). Alipay now allows foreign cards. WeChat Pay works the same way but the setup is slightly harder. Carry 500–1,000 CNY in cash as backup — some remote temples and village restaurants only take cash.

Pro tip: Set up Alipay at least a week before departure. Some cards require phone verification. If Alipay fails, install WeChat and WeChat Pay.

Internet: VPN and eSIM

Google, Gmail, Instagram, WhatsApp, and YouTube are blocked. You need a VPN installed BEFORE arriving — VPN websites are blocked inside China. Recommended: LetsVPN or Mullvad (both have worked reliably). Alternatively, buy a travel eSIM (Airalo, Nomad, Holafly) — these use Hong Kong networks and bypass the firewall entirely, so no VPN is needed.

Pro tip: An eSIM from a Hong Kong provider is the simplest option. Your apps will work as normal with no VPN. Download the eSIM before departure.

Navigation: Maps That Work

Google Maps does NOT work reliably in China — locations are offset by hundreds of meters and transit directions are wrong. Install Baidu Maps (百度地图, Chinese only) or Amap (高德地图, has some English labels). Apple Maps works correctly in China if you have an iPhone. Download offline maps before any mountain or rural trip.

Pro tip: Save all destinations in Chinese characters to your phone. Show them to taxi drivers. Learn to recognize: 入口 (entrance), 出口 (exit), 卫生间 (toilet), 售票处 (ticket office).

Language: Translation Tools

Outside international hotels and major airports, English is rare. Download Pleco (the best Chinese dictionary app) and Baidu Translate or Microsoft Translator with Chinese offline pack. Google Translate works with a VPN. At restaurants, point at items. At ticket counters, show saved Chinese text.

Pro tip: Pleco's optical character reader (OCR) translates signs through your phone camera. Worth paying for the upgrade. The phrase "Zhege" (这个, "this one") + pointing = 80% of what you need.

High-Speed Rail (HSR)

China's HSR network is the world's best. Book tickets on Trip.com (formerly Ctrip, works in English with foreign cards) or 12306.cn (official, Chinese only, foreign cards now accepted). Book 2–14 days ahead. At the station: arrive 1 hour early for security and ID check. Your passport is your ticket — scan it at the gate.

Pro tip: Trip.com is the easiest option for foreigners. First class (一等座) is worth the small price difference: wider seats, quieter car. Bring food — HSR dining cars are limited.

Booking Attractions: WeChat Mini-Programs

Most major attractions require advance booking through WeChat mini-programs. This is the single biggest surprise for first-time visitors. Key ones: Shaanxi History Museum (3–5 days ahead, free), Mogao Caves (2–3 weeks ahead, 238 CNY), Palace Museum (7 days ahead, 60 CNY). Bookings open at specific times — check each site.

Pro tip: If WeChat booking fails, use Trip.com or Klook for attraction tickets. They handle Chinese bookings with an English interface. You pay a small markup but save hours of frustration.

Tang and Song Ceramics: What to See and Where

Tang and Song ceramics represent the peak of Chinese pottery. Tang gave us bold sancai (three-color) glazes. Song gave us the five great kilns — subtle monochrome wares that imperial collectors still covet. Here is what matters and where to find it.

Tang Dynasty Ceramics (618–907)

Sancai (三彩) — Three-Color Glazed Pottery

Bold lead-glazed ceramics in amber, green, and cream. Used for tomb figures and vessels, not daily tableware. The most recognizable Tang art form. Famous camel and horse figurines show Silk Road influence.

Best collections: Shaanxi History Museum (Xi'an), Luoyang Museum (Henan), British Museum (London)

Yue Ware (越窑) — Celadon from the South

Green-glazed stoneware from Zhejiang kilns. Poets called it "the color of a thousand peaks." Tang tea master Lu Yu declared Yue ware the best for tea.

Best collections: Zhejiang Provincial Museum (Hangzhou), Shanghai Museum

Xing Ware (邢窑) — White Porcelain of the North

Northern white ware from Hebei. Lu Yu ranked it second to Yue, but the Tang court loved it. Precursor to the white porcelain tradition.

Best collections: Hebei Museum (Shijiazhuang), Palace Museum (Beijing)

Song Dynasty Ceramics (960–1279)

Ru Ware (汝窑) — The Holy Grail

Sky-blue glaze with a crackled surface like ice. Made for only ~20 years at the Northern Song court. Fewer than 100 pieces survive worldwide. At Sotheby's in 2017, a Ru brush washer sold for $37.7 million.

Where to see: National Palace Museum (Taipei, 21 pieces), Palace Museum (Beijing)

Guan Ware (官窑) — Official Imperial Kiln

Southern Song court ceramics from Hangzhou. Thick crackled glaze in shades of gray-blue and celadon. Fired at the kilns you can visit today in Hangzhou.

Where to see: Southern Song Official Kiln Museum (Hangzhou), National Palace Museum (Taipei)

Jun, Ge, and Ding Wares

Jun (钧): Purple-splashed blue glazes from Henan. Ge (哥): Deliberate crackle patterns in golden and black lines. Ding (定): Ivory-white porcelain, often with impressed floral designs — the first true white porcelain.

Best collections: Shanghai Museum, Palace Museum (Beijing), British Museum

Where to See the Best Ceramics in China

Shaanxi History Museum

Xi'an. World's finest Tang sancai collection. Free. Book 3+ days ahead on WeChat.

Southern Song Official Kiln Museum

Hangzhou. Built on the actual kiln site where Guan ware was fired for the Song emperor.

National Palace Museum

Taipei. 21 Ru ware pieces — the finest single collection. Also exceptional Guan, Ge, Jun, Ding.

Shanghai Museum

Outstanding ceramics gallery. Strong Jun and Ding collections. Free.

Tea Culture: From Tang Bricks to Song Whisking

China's tea culture was born in Tang and reached its most refined form in Song. If you love tea, these are the places to experience what started it all.

Lu Yu and The Classic of Tea

Lu Yu (陆羽, 733–804) was a Tang orphan raised by monks who became the world's first tea scholar. His book The Classic of Tea (《茶经》, c. 760) covers everything: where tea grows, how to pick it, what water to use, how to brew it, what cups to drink from. He declared Yue ware celadon the best tea bowl — it brought out the green of the tea.

Visit: Lu Yu Memorial Hall in Tianmen, Hubei (his hometown) or the China National Tea Museum in Hangzhou which has an excellent Lu Yu section.

Emperor Huizong's Treatise on Tea

Song Emperor Huizong (1082–1135) was a terrible ruler who lost half his empire, but a brilliant artist and tea connoisseur. His Treatise on Tea (《大观茶论》, 1107) describes dian cha (点茶): whisking finely ground powdered tea into a froth using a bamboo whisk. The quality of the foam determined the quality of the tea.

Visit: The Song dynasty tea ceremony is reconstructed at the China National Tea Museum in Hangzhou. Book a tea ceremony experience (80–150 CNY).

Jingshan Temple (径山寺)

Near Hangzhou, this Tang-era temple is where a formalized Buddhist tea ritual developed in the Song dynasty. Monks here cultivated tea, whisked it, and developed a ceremonial approach to serving it. The surrounding mountains still produce Jingshan tea (径山茶), a prized green tea.

Visit: 50km northwest of Hangzhou. Bus from Hangzhou West Bus Station (1.5h, 20 CNY). Temple admission: 20 CNY. Tea tastings available at the temple and surrounding tea farms.

China National Tea Museum (Hangzhou)

The world's best tea museum, in the hills above West Lake. Four buildings covering tea history, tea ceremony, tea processing, and tea culture. The Tang-Song gallery shows how tea changed from boiled bricks (Tang) to whisked powder (Song) to steeped leaves (Ming).

Visit: Longjing Road, Hangzhou. Free. Open 9am–4:30pm (closed Tuesdays). Combine with a walk through the surrounding Longjing tea terraces — China's most famous green tea grows here. Metro Line 1 to Ding'an Road, then taxi (15 min, 20 CNY).

Poetry Landmarks: Walk Where Tang and Song Poets Walked

Tang poetry and Song ci lyrics are the heart of Chinese literature. Li Bai drank under the moon. Du Fu watched his country fall. Su Shi wrote about pork belly and moonlight on the river. You can visit the places they immortalized.

Mount Emei (峨眉山) — Li Bai's Moon

Li Bai's most famous short poem begins: "The moon above Mount Emei / half a wheel of autumn." The mountain was a Tang-era Buddhist center and a place of pilgrimage. Today it's a UNESCO site and one of China's Four Sacred Buddhist Mountains. The Golden Summit (3077m) has a Tang-dynasty bronze temple.

Visit: Sichuan province. HSR from Chengdu to Emeishan (1.5h, 65 CNY). Cable car to summit. Budget 2 days.

Read: "The Moon over Mount Emei" (峨眉山月歌) by Li Bai

Yellow Crane Tower (黄鹤楼) — Li Bai's Silence

The tower in Wuhan was a Tang-era landmark where poets gathered. Cui Hao's poem about it was so perfect that Li Bai, visiting years later, wrote: "I would write, but Cui Hao has already said it all." The current tower is a 1985 reconstruction — but the view of the Yangtze is real.

Visit: Wuhan, Hubei. Metro Line 5 to Yellow Crane Tower Station. Admission: 70 CNY. Best at sunset.

West Lake (西湖) — Su Shi's Masterpiece

Su Shi (Su Dongpo) governed Hangzhou twice and transformed West Lake. He dredged it, built the Su Causeway (苏堤) lined with willow and peach trees, and wrote its most famous poem: "Compare West Lake to Xi Shi, the beauty / light makeup or heavy, always just right."

Visit: Hangzhou. Walk the Su Causeway at dawn for the "Spring Dawn on Su Causeway" view — one of the Ten Scenes of West Lake. Free.

Du Fu's Thatched Cottage (杜甫草堂) — Chengdu

Du Fu lived here from 760–765 after fleeing the An Lushan Rebellion. In this modest cottage he wrote over 240 poems — some of the greatest in Chinese literature. "My thatched roof torn by autumn wind" was written here. The current site is a Ming-Qing reconstruction with a beautiful bamboo garden.

Visit: Chengdu, Sichuan. Metro Line 4 to Caotang North Road. Admission: 60 CNY. Budget 2 hours. The garden is as rewarding as the cottage.

Red Cliffs (赤壁) — Su Shi's Imagination

Su Shi wrote his two "Odes to the Red Cliffs" about a boat trip on the Yangtze near Huangzhou (now Huanggang, Hubei). He was actually at the WRONG Red Cliffs — the historical battle site is 200km upstream. But Su Shi's poems made HIS cliffs the famous ones. Literary tourism at its finest.

Visit: Huanggang, Hubei. HSR from Wuhan (30 min). "Su Shi's Red Cliffs" park: 40 CNY.

Museums with Tang and Song Collections

Not everything is at the ruin sites. The best Tang and Song artifacts are in museums around the world. Here is where to find them.

CNXi'an

Shaanxi History Museum

Richest Tang gold, silver, and mural collection anywhere. The Tang dynasty gallery alone is worth a half-day.

Admission: Free (book 3+ days ahead on WeChat mini-program)

CNBeijing

Palace Museum

Holds the original Song painting Qingming Shanghe Tu and an unmatched imperial ceramics collection. The painting is rarely displayed.

Admission: 60 CNY (book 7 days ahead)

TWTaipei

National Palace Museum

Largest Song ceramics collection outside mainland China. 21 Ru ware pieces — the finest in the world.

Admission: 350 TWD

GBLondon

British Museum (Room 33)

Admonitions Scroll (Tang copy) and Tang-Song ceramics. The scroll is displayed for only ~6 weeks per year due to conservation.

Admission: Free

CNHangzhou

Southern Song Official Kiln Museum

The single best Tang material culture collection on earth — 9,000+ objects imported from Tang China.

Admission: Free

USWashington DC

Freer Gallery of Art

The Peacock Room installation includes Song ceramics. Strong Tang Buddhist sculpture and painting collection.

Admission: Free

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Tang Paradise in Xi'an original?

No. Built after 2000. It is a theme park. For real Tang sites in Xi'an: Big Wild Goose Pagoda (Ming rebuild on Tang foundation), Shaanxi History Museum, and the Daming Palace archaeological park.

Is Qingming Riverside Garden worth visiting?

Only with family. It is a 1998 theme park with zero historical fabric. For real Song structures: Baoguo Temple (1013, Ningbo) or Iron Pagoda (1049, Kaifeng).

How long should I plan for a Tang-Song trip?

Minimum 2 weeks for both trails. 8-10 days for Tang alone (Xi'an, Taiyuan, Dunhuang). 10-14 days for Song alone (Kaifeng, Suzhou, Hangzhou, Ningbo). See the Routes section for detailed itineraries.

Are sites accessible without Chinese language skills?

Mixed. Xi'an and Hangzhou have good infrastructure. Shanxi mountain temples (Foguang, Nanchan) have almost no English signage and limited transit. Hire a guide or join a specialized tour. See our Travel Tips section for apps and preparation advice.

What is the oldest wooden building in China?

Nanchan Temple in Shanxi, dated 782 (Tang Dynasty). It survived the great Buddhist persecution of 845 because it was too remote and insignificant to target.

Can I visit Foguang Temple without a guide?

Yes, but it is difficult. The temple is 4 hours from Taiyuan on mountain roads. No public transit. You need a private driver (~600-800 CNY/day from Taiyuan). Combine with Nanchan Temple in one day. No English signage.

What should I know about visiting Mogao Caves?

Book Standard Visit (8 caves, 238 CNY) 2-3 weeks ahead via WeChat mini-program. Strictly timed tours. No photos inside. Best months: April-May and September-October.

Which museum has the best Tang and Song collection?

Shaanxi History Museum (Xi'an) has the best Tang gold and silver. National Palace Museum (Taipei) has the best Song ceramics (21 Ru ware pieces). Shanghai Museum has outstanding ceramics. See our Ceramics and Museums sections.

Do I need a VPN in China?

Yes for Google/Gmail/Instagram/WhatsApp. Install a VPN BEFORE arriving in China. Alternatively, a Hong Kong travel eSIM bypasses the firewall entirely. See our Travel Tips section for detailed instructions.

How do I pay for things in China?

Download Alipay and link your international credit card before departure. WeChat Pay also works. Carry 500-1,000 CNY in cash as backup for remote temples and village restaurants. See Travel Tips for setup instructions.
