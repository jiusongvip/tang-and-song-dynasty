How to Tell Real Ruins from Replicas

China markets many "ancient" attractions built in the last 30 years. Here is how to tell the difference before you spend time and money on a theme park.

Quick Checklist

  1. Check the basis. Is it a National Protected Unit (NPU)? NPU means state-verified authenticity. Look for a stone plaque near the entrance.
  2. Look at materials. Real old wood is dark, cracked, irregular. Painted concrete and uniform brickwork = modern.
  3. Check the ticket price. Real historic sites: 20-60 CNY. Theme parks: 120-300 CNY.
  4. Is there a costume gift shop? If people are renting Tang costumes and taking photos, you are probably in a theme park. Real sites have a ticket booth and not much else.
  5. Google the construction date. If it says "built 1998" or "built 2010," trust that number. It is not ancient.

When Reconstructions Are Worth It

Not all reconstructions are a waste. Qingming Riverside Garden (1998) gives you a walkable model of Song street life based on a famous painting. If you are traveling with children, it is engaging.

Tang Paradise in Xi'an is pleasant for an evening stroll and photos. It is not a historical site. Treat it like a Renaissance fair — fun, but do not confuse it with the real thing.
