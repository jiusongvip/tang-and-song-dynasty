Tea Culture: From Tang Bricks to Song Whisking

China's tea culture was born in Tang and reached its most refined form in Song. If you love tea, these are the places to experience what started it all.

Lu Yu and The Classic of Tea

Lu Yu (陆羽, 733–804) was a Tang orphan raised by monks who became the world's first tea scholar. His book The Classic of Tea (《茶经》, c. 760) covers everything: where tea grows, how to pick it, what water to use, how to brew it, what cups to drink from. He declared Yue ware celadon the best tea bowl — it brought out the green of the tea.

Visit: Lu Yu Memorial Hall in Tianmen, Hubei (his hometown) or the China National Tea Museum in Hangzhou which has an excellent Lu Yu section.

Emperor Huizong's Treatise on Tea

Song Emperor Huizong (1082–1135) was a terrible ruler who lost half his empire, but a brilliant artist and tea connoisseur. His Treatise on Tea (《大观茶论》, 1107) describes dian cha (点茶): whisking finely ground powdered tea into a froth using a bamboo whisk. The quality of the foam determined the quality of the tea.

Visit: The Song dynasty tea ceremony is reconstructed at the China National Tea Museum in Hangzhou. Book a tea ceremony experience (80–150 CNY).

Jingshan Temple (径山寺)

Near Hangzhou, this Tang-era temple is where a formalized Buddhist tea ritual developed in the Song dynasty. Monks here cultivated tea, whisked it, and developed a ceremonial approach to serving it. The surrounding mountains still produce Jingshan tea (径山茶), a prized green tea.

Visit: 50km northwest of Hangzhou. Bus from Hangzhou West Bus Station (1.5h, 20 CNY). Temple admission: 20 CNY. Tea tastings available at the temple and surrounding tea farms.

China National Tea Museum (Hangzhou)

One of the most comprehensive tea museums, in the hills above West Lake. Four buildings covering tea history, tea ceremony, tea processing, and tea culture. The Tang-Song gallery shows how tea changed from boiled bricks (Tang) to whisked powder (Song) to steeped leaves (Ming).

Visit: Longjing Road, Hangzhou. Free. Open 9am–4:30pm (closed Tuesdays). Combine with a walk through the surrounding Longjing tea terraces — China's most famous green tea grows here. Metro Line 1 to Ding'an Road, then taxi (15 min, 20 CNY).
