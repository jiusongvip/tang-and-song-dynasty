Traveling in China: Practical Guide for First-Timers

China is not difficult to travel, but it's different. The language barrier is real, and China's digital ecosystem can lock you out before you even land. Here is everything you need to navigate it.

Payments: Alipay and WeChat Pay

Cash is rarely accepted. Download Alipay before your trip and link your international credit card (Visa/Mastercard). Alipay now allows foreign cards. WeChat Pay works the same way but the setup is slightly harder. Carry 500–1,000 CNY in cash as backup — some remote temples and village restaurants only take cash.

Pro tip: Set up Alipay at least a week before departure. Some cards require phone verification. If Alipay fails, install WeChat and WeChat Pay.

Internet: VPN and eSIM

Google, Gmail, Instagram, WhatsApp, and YouTube are blocked. You need a VPN installed BEFORE arriving — VPN websites are blocked inside China. Recommended: LetsVPN or Mullvad (both have worked reliably). Alternatively, buy a travel eSIM (Airalo, Nomad, Holafly) — these use Hong Kong networks and bypass the firewall entirely, so no VPN is needed.

Pro tip: An eSIM from a Hong Kong provider is the simplest option. Your apps will work as normal with no VPN. Download the eSIM before departure.

Navigation: Maps That Work

Google Maps does NOT work reliably in China — locations are offset by hundreds of meters and transit directions are wrong. Install Baidu Maps (百度地图, Chinese only) or Amap (高德地图, has some English labels). Apple Maps works correctly in China if you have an iPhone. Download offline maps before any mountain or rural trip.

Pro tip: Save all destinations in Chinese characters to your phone. Show them to taxi drivers. Learn to recognize: 入口 (entrance), 出口 (exit), 卫生间 (toilet), 售票处 (ticket office).

Language: Translation Tools

Outside international hotels and major airports, English is rare. Download Pleco (the best Chinese dictionary app) and Baidu Translate or Microsoft Translator with Chinese offline pack. Google Translate works with a VPN. At restaurants, point at items. At ticket counters, show saved Chinese text.

Pro tip: Pleco's optical character reader (OCR) translates signs through your phone camera. Worth paying for the upgrade. The phrase "Zhege" (这个, "this one") + pointing = 80% of what you need.

High-Speed Rail (HSR)

China's HSR network is the world's best. Book tickets on Trip.com (formerly Ctrip, works in English with foreign cards) or 12306.cn (official, Chinese only, foreign cards now accepted). Book 2–14 days ahead. At the station: arrive 1 hour early for security and ID check. Your passport is your ticket — scan it at the gate.

Pro tip: Trip.com is the easiest option for foreigners. First class (一等座) is worth the small price difference: wider seats, quieter car. Bring food — HSR dining cars are limited.

Booking Attractions: WeChat Mini-Programs

Most major attractions require advance booking through WeChat mini-programs. This is the single biggest surprise for first-time visitors. Key ones: Shaanxi History Museum (3–5 days ahead, free), Mogao Caves (2–3 weeks ahead, 238 CNY), Palace Museum (7 days ahead, 60 CNY). Bookings open at specific times — check each site.

Pro tip: If WeChat booking fails, use Trip.com or Klook for attraction tickets. They handle Chinese bookings with an English interface. You pay a small markup but save hours of frustration.
