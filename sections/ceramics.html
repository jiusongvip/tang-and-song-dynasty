Tang and Song Ceramics: What to See and Where

Tang and Song ceramics represent the peak of Chinese pottery. Tang gave us bold sancai (three-color) glazes. Song gave us the five great kilns — subtle monochrome wares that imperial collectors still covet. Here is what matters and where to find it.

Tang Dynasty Ceramics (618–907)

Sancai (三彩) — Three-Color Glazed Pottery

Bold lead-glazed ceramics in amber, green, and cream. Used for tomb figures and vessels, not daily tableware. The most recognizable Tang art form. Famous camel and horse figurines show Silk Road influence.

Best collections: Shaanxi History Museum (Xi'an), Luoyang Museum (Henan), British Museum (London)

Yue Ware (越窑) — Celadon from the South

Green-glazed stoneware from Zhejiang kilns. Poets called it "the color of a thousand peaks." Tang tea master Lu Yu declared Yue ware the best for tea.

Best collections: Zhejiang Provincial Museum (Hangzhou), Shanghai Museum

Xing Ware (邢窑) — White Porcelain of the North

Northern white ware from Hebei. Lu Yu ranked it second to Yue, but the Tang court loved it. Precursor to the white porcelain tradition.

Best collections: Hebei Museum (Shijiazhuang), Palace Museum (Beijing)

Song Dynasty Ceramics (960–1279)

Ru Ware (汝窑) — The Rarest of All

Sky-blue glaze with a crackled surface like ice. Made for only ~20 years at the Northern Song court. Fewer than 100 pieces survive worldwide. At Sotheby's in 2017, a Ru brush washer sold for $37.7 million.

Where to see: National Palace Museum (Taipei, 21 pieces), Palace Museum (Beijing)

Guan Ware (官窑) — Official Imperial Kiln

Southern Song court ceramics from Hangzhou. Thick crackled glaze in shades of gray-blue and celadon. Fired at the kilns you can visit today in Hangzhou.

Where to see: Southern Song Official Kiln Museum (Hangzhou), National Palace Museum (Taipei)

Jun, Ge, and Ding Wares

Jun (钧): Purple-splashed blue glazes from Henan. Ge (哥): Deliberate crackle patterns in golden and black lines. Ding (定): Ivory-white porcelain, often with impressed floral designs — the first true white porcelain.

Best collections: Shanghai Museum, Palace Museum (Beijing), British Museum

Where to See the Best Ceramics in China

Shaanxi History Museum

Xi'an. World's finest Tang sancai collection. Free. Book 3+ days ahead on WeChat.

Southern Song Official Kiln Museum

Hangzhou. Built on the actual kiln site where Guan ware was fired for the Song emperor.

National Palace Museum

Taipei. 21 Ru ware pieces — the finest single collection. Also exceptional Guan, Ge, Jun, Ding.

Shanghai Museum

Outstanding ceramics gallery. Strong Jun and Ding collections. Free.
