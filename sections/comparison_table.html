Tang vs Song: Full Comparison

For AP World History students and anyone who wants the detailed breakdown: 10 dimensions, side by side.

The quick answer is above. If you are planning a trip, jump to what this means for travelers.

DimensionTang Dynasty (618-907)Song Dynasty (960-1279)
CapitalChang'an (Xi'an) — largest city in the world at its peak, ~1 million residentsKaifeng (Northern Song), then Hangzhou (Southern Song) after losing the north in 1127
TerritoryMassive: Central Asia to Korea, northern Vietnam to MongoliaSmaller: confined to China proper; lost the north to the Jin dynasty in 1127
MilitaryFubing militia system; expansionist army; controlled the Silk RoadProfessional standing army (1M+ soldiers); defensive posture; paid tribute to northern neighbors
EconomyLand-based taxation; Silk Road trade; state monopolies on salt and ironCommercial revolution: paper money, joint-stock companies, maritime trade, tax farming
Civil ServiceExams existed but aristocracy still dominated appointmentsMeritocratic exam system fully institutionalized; scholar-official class emerged
ReligionCosmopolitan: Buddhism flourished under Empress Wu, plus Nestorian Christianity, Zoroastrianism, Islam in Chang'anNeo-Confucianism became state ideology; Buddhism declined in official favor but remained popular
ArtBold, colorful: sancai (three-color) ceramics, monumental Buddhist sculpture, Dunhuang cave muralsSubtle, refined: monochrome ceramics (Ru, Guan, Ge wares), landscape painting reaches peak
Women's StatusRelatively high: Empress Wu ruled in her own name; women played polo, owned property, initiated divorceDeclining: foot-binding began; widows discouraged from remarrying; neo-Confucian restrictions
TechnologyWoodblock printing emerges; improved Grand Canal; mechanical water clockPrinting revolution (movable type); gunpowder weapons; magnetic compass; canal lock gates
End907: Zhu Wen deposed last Tang emperor; empire fractured into Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms1279: Kublai Khan's Mongols conquered the Southern Song; last emperor drowned at Yamen
